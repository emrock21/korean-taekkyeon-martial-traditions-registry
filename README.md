# Korean Taekkyeon Martial Traditions Registry

This repository preserves, in an immutable and decentralized format, the martial traditions of **Taekkyeon**. Korea’s fluid, rhythmic, and dance-like martial art recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Taekkyeon is characterized by **pumbalki** (continuous stepping), **circular kicks**, **sweeps**, and **hwalgaejil** (wide arm gestures). Unlike linear striking arts, Taekkyeon emphasizes rhythm, flow, and non-linear movement.

## Why Taekkyeon Matters

Taekkyeon represents:

- a **unique movement philosophy**, based on circularity and rhythm  
- a **surviving martial lineage**, preserved through master Song Deok-gi  
- a **regional diversity**, with Seoul, Jeolla, and Gyeonggi variations  
- a **ritual and competitive tradition**, performed in festivals and matches  
- a **UNESCO-recognized heritage**, inscribed in 2011  

Its fluidity and musicality distinguish it from all other East Asian martial arts.

## Purpose of This Contract

The goal of this registry is to:

1. **Create a permanent, censorship-resistant record** of Taekkyeon traditions.  
2. **Preserve endangered martial knowledge** with technical precision.  
3. **Document styles, techniques, footwork, and cultural context**.  
4. **Provide an immutable reference** for researchers, practitioners, and future generations.  
5. **Honor the regional and stylistic diversity of Taekkyeon.**

The contract is intentionally:

- **ownerless** — no authority can alter or censor the registry  
- **payment-free** — no monetization, no barriers  
- **minimalistic and secure** — no external calls  
- **permanent** — entries become part of the cultural ledger  

## What Each Entry Records

- **styleName** — Gyeongdang, Jeolla style, UNESCO Taekkyeon  
- **techniques** — kicks, sweeps, circular motions  
- **footwork** — pumbalki, evasive stepping  
- **region** — Seoul, Jeolla, Gyeonggi  
- **culturalContext** — festivals, competitions, rituals  
- **historicalNotes** — suppression, revival, lineage  
- **uniqueness** — fluidity, rhythm, UNESCO status
